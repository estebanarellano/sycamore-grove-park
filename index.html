Project Overview

What We're Doing

We are working in community to develop a shared vision for Sycamore Grove Park and Ramona Hall Community Center, where neighborhood stability, climate resilience, accessibility, celebration, play, and connection to the rich landscape of Los Angeles can inform the future of these shared public resources.

The Master Plan will be a guidebook for that future: a set of recommendations, from small fixes like a working water fountain to larger changes to the landscape, that support the people, plants, and animals who use the park. It is the first master plan for Sycamore Grove to center the voice of the local community, and community input will shape both documents the process produces: the Master Plan and an Environmental Impact Report.

The planning process will follow five phases.

  1. I

    Phase I

    Research and Analysis

    Explore Sycamore Grove's history and existing conditions, and gather community and stakeholder insights to identify opportunities, challenges, and priorities that will help guide the Master Plan.

    Current
  2. II

    Phase II

    Design Alternatives

    Develop different ideas for the park and identify short and long-term improvement priorities based on community and stakeholder input.

  3. III

    Phase III

    Master Plan Preparation

    Refine the preferred ideas and compile all research, community outreach findings, and design recommendations into a comprehensive vision for the park.

  4. IV

    Phase IV

    Environmental Review

    Complete environmental review in accordance with the California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA) to support project approval.

  5. V

    Phase V

    Master Plan Adoption

    Once approved, the Master Plan will guide the future of the park.

Then and Now

A Grove Shaped by Water

The Western Sycamore, after which the park is named, has long provided shade and shelter with its mottled bark and wide canopy. Sycamores thrive in moist ground, and the water that fed this grove came from the North Branch creek, which was buried in the early twentieth century.

Historic photos and neighbors' stories will appear here beside views of the park today, so everyone can start the conversation from the same place.

About the Site

Sycamore Grove Today

Sycamore Grove is both a city park and a riparian environment, located immediately adjacent to the Arroyo Seco and the Arroyo Seco Parkway.

The park is a beloved community asset, with the historic Ramona Hall Community Center, a bandshell, tennis courts, and other amenities that serve surrounding Highland Park residents.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

The Sycamore Grove Park Master Plan is a planning initiative led by the City of Los Angeles Department of Recreation and Parks to guide the future of Sycamore Grove Park and Ramona Hall Community Center. It is the first master plan for the park to center the voice of the local community.

A master plan is a legal document: a set of recommendations for how a park should change over time. Recommendations can range from small improvements, like fixing a water fountain, to larger changes to the landscape.

This process produces two documents: the Master Plan and an Environmental Impact Report, which studies how proposed changes would affect the environment. Community input shapes the decisions in both.
