The Park01

Sycamore Grove is a beloved community park with a long history of serving Highland Park residents. It is one of Los Angeles’ oldest city parks, and a valued site for celebration, exercise, and play.

After more than 120 years as an official Los Angeles City Park, and many more as ancestral land of the Tongva people, the park is ready for a new vision that taps its full potential for the community and nature.

The park is a critical connection to the Arroyo Seco watershed.

While the park’s namesake sycamores have long provided cool shade for residents, they thrive in a moist environment originally created by North Branch Creek. Over the 20th century, this tributary was buried and eventually redirected to the municipal stormwater system because of high contaminant levels.

Ramona Hall has not been upgraded since [year], and reviving the creek and modernizing the park’s amenities could help many future generations continue to enjoy this unique spot.

The Plan02

What the Master Plan is, who is shaping it, and what comes first.

  1. Sycamore Grove Master PlanNo. 01

    A guidebook for the future

    The Master Plan will be a guidebook for that future: a set of recommendations, from small fixes like a working water fountain to larger changes to the landscape, that support the people, plants, and animals who use the park.

  3. Sycamore Grove Master PlanNo. 02

    Two documents

    It is the first master plan for Sycamore Grove to center the voice of the local community. Community input will shape both documents the process produces: the Master Plan and an Environmental Impact Report.

  4. Sycamore Grove Master PlanNo. 03

    A team that listens

    Los Angeles Recreation and Parks has engaged a team of community organizers, designers, and ecologists whose primary goal is to listen to stakeholders who use, live around, and benefit from the park, and then create a shovel-ready master plan that incorporates recreation, education, and the environment.

    The plan will outline park changes that best support people, plants, and animals. The local community’s voice will drive the plan through surveys, community meetings, and other forms of engagement.

  6. Sycamore Grove Master PlanNo. 04

    Planning comes first

    The first step toward updating the park is the planning and environmental review processes. Implementation of the master plan will be part of the conversation, but it will be part of the second phase.

    We are excited to work with stakeholders and look forward to envisioning a new chapter for Sycamore Grove together.

Photos: Rio Asch Phoenix

Process & Timeline03

The Master Plan will be developed over approximately 24 months through three primary phases.

  1. /01We are here

    Phase I Discovery

    Research & Analysis

    Documenting existing site conditions, historic and cultural resources, infrastructure, and ecology while gathering initial community stories, priorities, and concerns.

  2. /02

    Phase II Engagement

    Design Alternatives

    Developing and testing multiple conceptual design scenarios for Sycamore Grove Park and Ramona Hall based on community feedback and technical findings.

    Draft Master Plan Preparation

    Refining the preferred vision into a comprehensive Draft Master Plan with prioritized projects, cost estimates, and a phased implementation strategy.

  3. /03

    Phase III Development

    Final Master Plan Adoption

    Presenting the Final Master Plan to the Board of Recreation and Park Commissioners for formal adoption to guide future capital investments.

Alongside

The Environmental Impact Report will be developed in tandem with the Master Plan, following a similar but more structured process to complete required environmental assessments in compliance with the California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA) and tribal consultation (AB 52).

FAQ05

Why are we creating a Master Plan now?

While Sycamore Grove Park and Ramona Hall have received targeted upgrades over the years, such as the 2021 Measure A playground, the site lacks a unified, long-term roadmap. This Master Plan ensures future investments are coordinated, sustainable, and driven by community priorities.

How is this different from the walkway and restroom construction happening in the park?

The $1.1 million Measure A project currently underway focuses on immediate repairs: repaving walkways, connecting picnic paths, and upgrading restrooms to ADA standards by Fall 2027. The Master Plan looks much broader and further ahead, evaluating Ramona Hall, the bandshell, tennis courts, shade canopy, and long-term park transformation.

When will new Master Plan projects be built?

A Master Plan is a strategic blueprint, not a construction contract. Once the City adopts the Final Master Plan, it will use its phased recommendations to pursue dedicated capital funding for design and construction.

How can local schools, youth, and community groups get involved?

The project team is partnering with local schools, neighborhood councils, disability advocates, and cultural organizations throughout Highland Park and the Arroyo Seco corridor. To request a pop-up or focus group for your organization, contact the Planning Division.
